# HR Analytics and Employee Attrition

## Project Overview

This project explores employee departure patterns to help an HR team identify groups requiring further investigation and develop targeted retention initiatives.

The analysis covers 1,470 employee profiles, including 237 recorded departures, representing 16.12% of the dataset.

## Business Questions

- How is the workforce distributed across departments, roles and demographic groups?
- Which departments and roles have the highest departure proportions?
- How do departures vary with tenure, overtime, satisfaction, travel and income?
- Which employee groups should HR prioritise for further investigation?

## Tools and Approach

- **Power Query:** checked data types, created age, tenure, distance and income groups, and added numerical sorting columns.
- **DAX:** calculated employee counts, departure counts and departure proportions.
- **Power BI:** built three interactive dashboard pages with filters, tooltips and reset buttons.
- ** ChatGPT:** Used to write the report and help in drawing the recommendations
- **Claude:** Used to create the PowerPoint Presentations.
  

### Dashboard Pages

1. **Workforce Overview:** employee distribution by department, role, age, gender and tenure.
2. **Attrition Overview:** departure proportions by department, role and company tenure, including comparisons of group size and departure proportion.
3. **Work Experience & Career:** associations between departures and working conditions, income and career history.

## Key Insights

- **Overtime:** profiles with overtime recorded 30.53% departures, compared with 10.44% without overtime.
- **Early tenure:** employees with 0–1 year at the company recorded 34.88% departures, compared with 11.07% among those with 5–9 years.
- **Job roles:** Sales Representatives had the highest departure proportion at 39.76%. Laboratory Technicians and Sales Executives together accounted for 50.21% of recorded departures.
- **Business travel:** Frequent travellers recorded 24.91% departures, compared with 8.00% among non-travellers.
- **Income:** the lowest income band recorded 28.61% departures. However, comparisons within the same role and level showed that the relationship varied.
- **Career progression:** Longer intervals since the last promotion did not show a higher departure proportion.

## Recommendations

- Investigate workload and staffing in teams with frequent overtime.
- Strengthen onboarding through structured check-ins and support during the first year.
- Conduct role-specific retention reviews for Sales Representatives and Laboratory Technicians.
- Review compensation within comparable roles and job levels before proposing adjustments.
- Explore travel, scheduling and commuting support through targeted pilots.
- Collect employment dates, exit reasons and career history to evaluate retention outcomes over time.

The **report and PowerPoint presentation** answer all ten analytical questions in detail, explain each visualisation, and present **16 recommendations** with proposed owners, timelines, and evaluation measures.

## Interpretation

The departure proportions describe this dataset and should not be interpreted as annual turnover rates because no reporting period is available. The findings show associations, not proven causes. Survey scale definitions, income currency and other field meanings require confirmation before operational decisions.
